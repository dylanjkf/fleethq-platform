# Australian Compliance

## Purpose
Give Australian transport businesses confidence that FleetOS keeps them on the right side of regulation, without needing a separate compliance folder or spreadsheet alongside the software.

## Scope (v1, Australia only)
- **NHVR (National Heavy Asset Regulator) requirements** relevant to the target customer segment (courier/light-to-medium commercial fleets), including asset standards and registration obligations.
- **Chain of Responsibility (CoR)** — tracking and evidencing that scheduling, loading, and asset condition decisions meet CoR obligations, using Timeline data (checklists, maintenance records, dispatch decisions) as the evidence trail rather than a separate paper process.
- **Fatigue management / rest-break rules** — tracking operator work/rest hours against applicable standard hours rules, surfacing upcoming breaches before they happen (a Fleet Intelligence-assisted alert, with the underlying rule check itself being deterministic, not AI-guessed).
- **Registration, insurance, and roadworthy currency** — via Digital Glovebox expiry tracking.

## Requirements
- All jurisdiction-specific rules are implemented behind the Jurisdiction Model (`Jurisdiction_Model.md`) so they are swappable/extendable, not hardcoded through the compliance service.
- Every compliance-relevant action (checklist completion, fatigue-rule check, document expiry) produces a Timeline event, making an audit trail a byproduct of normal use, not a special export process.
- Compliance dashboard in FleetHQ surfaces current risk state (upcoming expiries, operators approaching fatigue limits, assets with open compliance-relevant faults) prominently, not buried in a report.

## Edge cases
- An operator working across a state boundary or multiple work diaries: rules must be evaluated correctly for the applicable standard, not assumed uniform nationally.
- Manual override of a system-flagged compliance risk (e.g. a manager consciously dispatches an operator close to an hours limit): must be permitted (this is the business's legal call, not the software's to block) but logged clearly as an overridden warning, not silently allowed.

## Technical considerations
- Fatigue/hours rule logic should be implemented as clearly defined, testable business rules (not embedded in UI code), since these rules are the kind of thing that changes with regulation updates and needs to be auditable and correct independent of interface changes.

## Acceptance criteria
- A compliance officer can see, in one dashboard, every asset and operator currently at risk (expiry, fatigue, open compliance fault) without cross-referencing separate systems.
- Every fatigue-rule override is distinctly logged as an override, never indistinguishable from a compliant state.

## Future expansion notes
- Additional Australian regulatory detail (dangerous goods handling, mass/loading limits for heavier asset classes) is a natural extension once the courier-segment core is proven, ahead of any international expansion.

## Implementation notes (Compliance milestone, `apps/api` + `apps/fleethq`)
- Built a deliberately scoped slice of this file's own "Registration, insurance, and roadworthy currency" line: a `ComplianceDocument` entity per Asset (`REGISTRATION`/`INSURANCE`/`ROADWORTHY`), with an expiry status (`valid`/`expiring_soon`/`expired`, 30-day window) computed at read time from `expiresAt` — never stored, per the "zero duplicate data entry" principle. NHVR requirements, Chain of Responsibility evidencing, and fatigue/hours tracking are **not** built in this slice — fatigue tracking specifically has no data source yet (no operator work/rest-hours clock exists until DriverOS ships), and CoR evidencing is a cross-entity reporting view better built once more of the underlying Timeline data (checklists, dispatch decisions) exists to evidence.
- The FleetHQ Compliance page (a document list sorted by soonest-expiring first) is a narrower stand-in for this file's "compliance dashboard" requirement — it satisfies the document-expiry portion of the acceptance criterion ("every asset... at risk... without cross-referencing separate systems") but not the fatigue or open-compliance-fault portions, since neither data source exists yet. Not cross-referenced with Maintenance's open-fault data in this slice, unlike Dispatch's assign dialog — a natural next step once both modules' read-model-join pattern is worth repeating here too.
- `ComplianceDocument` follows the Asset/Operator/AttachedUnit `archivedAt` reference-record pattern, not the Job/MaintenanceJob terminal-status pattern — a document has no natural terminal lifecycle of its own (renewing one means logging a new document, not mutating the old one), so archiving is purely a mistake-correction action. See `11-Database/Data_Model.md`.
- Added `compliance:view/create/edit/archive` to the permission catalog — see `14-Security/Permissions_Model.md`'s implementation notes for why this doesn't yet include a distinct "Approve" or "Audit" action from this file's own Compliance permission example list (no approval workflow or distinct audit action exists for a document record in this slice; viewing already provides audit visibility via Timeline).

## Implementation notes (Fatigue/Hours tracking, `apps/api` + `apps/fleethq` + `apps/driveros`)
- **Built**: this file's fatigue/rest-break line, unblocked now that `OperatorShift` (start/end timestamps, shipped in the Dispatch milestone) gives the rule engine a real work/rest-hours clock to evaluate — the exact blocker this doc's earlier Compliance-milestone note called out. `08-Compliance/Jurisdiction_Model.md`'s abstraction is real, not aspirational: `FatigueService` (`apps/api/src/compliance/fatigue.service.ts`) is jurisdiction-blind and contains no AU-specific number; it resolves a company's `jurisdiction` column to a `FatigueRuleSet` via a small registry (`compliance/jurisdiction/jurisdiction-registry.ts`) and only ever calls that rule set's own `evaluate()`. A company in an unregistered jurisdiction gets `not_assessed` back, per this doc's own "must be modeled as present-or-absent per jurisdiction" edge case — never silently evaluated against AU's numbers.
- **AU Standard Hours (solo driver) — v1 simplification** (`compliance/jurisdiction/au-fatigue-rules.ts`): four checks against a trailing window — max 12h work in 24h, minimum 7h continuous rest in 24h, max 72h work in 7 days, and minimum 24h continuous rest in 7 days (the fourth added by the `08-Compliance/NHVR_Compliance_Review.md` commercial-launch-readiness review — a distinct weekly rest requirement research turned up that the original three-check v1 didn't cover, meaning an operator could pass all three original checks while still working a full week with no real day off) — each with a status of `ok` / `approaching_limit` (within 60 minutes of the limit) / `breach`. **Deliberately not modeled**: Basic/Advanced Fatigue Management (BFM/AFM) alternative schemes, two-up crewing, the exact minute-level rest-break cadence within a single shift (e.g. sub-shift minimum rest at the 5.5h/8h/11h marks), the specific 10pm-8am "night rest break" timing definition, the 4-night-rest-breaks-per-14-days pattern rule, graduated minor/substantial/severe/critical breach severity, and the multi-work-diary/state-boundary edge case this doc names explicitly. These are real regulatory detail beyond what this slice models; see `NHVR_Compliance_Review.md` for the full list of what's confirmed vs. still needs a transport lawyer's sign-off against the primary legislative text (this session's automated research was blocked from directly fetching the primary Schedule and relied on secondary sources instead). The exact thresholds are constants in one file, not scattered through UI code, so they're auditable and adjustable independent of everything else — per this doc's own Technical Considerations.
- **Timeline as the audit trail**: a breach is written as a Timeline event on the Operator the moment a shift ends and pushes them over a limit (`ShiftsService.end()` calls `FatigueService.recordBreachOnShiftEnd()` in the same transaction) — "approaching" is never logged this way, only live-queried, so shift-end doesn't produce noise for a state that isn't actually a breach yet.
- **The override edge case is built**: `POST /v1/jobs/:id/assign` now checks the target operator's live fatigue status. A `breach` or `approaching_limit` status blocks the assign with `FATIGUE_RISK_UNACKNOWLEDGED` (409) unless the request sets `acknowledgeFatigueRisk: true`, in which case it proceeds **and** writes a distinct `fatigue_risk_overridden` Timeline event on the Operator — satisfying "logged clearly as an overridden warning, never indistinguishable from a compliant state." Reuses the existing `dispatch:assign` permission rather than a new one, since this is a state change on the same assign action, not a separate capability.
- **FleetHQ**: `AssignJobDialog` fetches the selected operator's live status and shows the risk + an explicit "assign anyway" checkbox inline (mirroring the existing open-critical-maintenance-fault warning on the Asset picker) rather than only surfacing the 409 after a failed submit — the Save button stays disabled until acknowledged when a risk exists. Compliance's page gets a second tab, "Fatigue," listing every currently at-risk operator (`GET /v1/fatigue/at-risk`, gated `compliance:view`, the same permission the rest of this dashboard already uses).
- **DriverOS**: the operator sees their own status via `GET /v1/fatigue/me` (no permission gate — the same "reads their own context" precedent as notification preferences), surfaced as a warning card on Today only when it's not `ok`. No offline cache fallback for this one read, unlike Today's job list or Checklist templates — a stale fatigue reading computed against a past "now" would be actively misleading rather than merely outdated, so the card just doesn't render if the request fails.
- **Compliance dashboard acceptance criterion**: still only two of the three factors this doc names ("expiry, fatigue, open compliance fault") are joined into one view — the Fatigue tab and the Documents tab are separate tabs on the same page, not a single unified risk feed, and neither cross-references Maintenance's open-fault data yet (the same gap the Compliance-milestone note above already flagged for documents). NHVR asset-standards checks and Chain-of-Responsibility evidencing remain unbuilt.

## Implementation notes (Chain of Responsibility evidencing, `apps/api` + `apps/fleethq`)
- **Built**: this doc's CoR line, unblocked for the reason the Compliance-milestone note above named ("better built once more of the underlying Timeline data exists to evidence") — Dispatch decisions, Smart Checklists, Maintenance, and Fatigue/Hours have all since shipped, so there's now a real body of Timeline/entity data to assemble. `CorEvidenceService` (`apps/api/src/compliance/cor-evidence.service.ts`) builds, for one Job, a read-only "evidence pack": the scheduling decisions (that Job's own Timeline events — created/assigned/reassigned/completed/cancelled), the assigned operator's fitness-for-duty signal (`fatigue_breach`/`fatigue_risk_overridden` Timeline events on the Operator during the job's active window), and the assigned asset's condition (checklist submissions during that window, any maintenance fault open at some point during it, and every compliance document's validity **evaluated as of the job's own window**, not "now" — a document that has since expired shouldn't retroactively look non-compliant for a job that ran while it was still valid, and one that's since been renewed shouldn't retroactively look compliant for a job that ran while it had actually lapsed).
- **"Loading" is explicitly out of scope**: this doc's CoR line also names loading/scheduling mass-and-dimension obligations — not modeled, per this doc's own Future expansion note deferring mass/loading limits to when heavier Asset Classes exist. The evidence pack covers scheduling and asset-condition duties only.
- **Nothing new is stored** — the pack is assembled at read time entirely from existing tables (`Job`, `TimelineEvent`, `ChecklistSubmission`, `MaintenanceJob`, `ComplianceDocument`), the same "zero duplicate data entry" reasoning as every other computed-status feature in this codebase (Fleet Health, Compliance expiry, Fatigue status). `GET /v1/compliance/cor-evidence/:jobId`, gated on the existing `compliance:view` permission — viewing CoR evidence is the same capability as viewing any other compliance data, not a new one.
- **FleetHQ**: a `CorEvidencePanel` drawer (mirroring `TimelinePanel`/`GraphPanel`'s per-entity shape) opened via a "CoR Evidence" row action on Dispatch, with a top-level "flagged concerns / no concerns" banner plus one section per evidence category. Works for jobs in any status, including completed/cancelled ones — CoR evidence is exactly the kind of thing pulled up after the fact (an audit, an incident investigation), not just while a job is active.
- **Deliberately not built**: NHVR asset-standards checks (registration/insurance/roadworthy currency already has its own slice via `ComplianceDocument`; this doc's broader "asset standards" beyond document currency is unaddressed) and any export/PDF generation of the evidence pack — it's a live view today, not yet a downloadable artifact for handing to a regulator or auditor.
